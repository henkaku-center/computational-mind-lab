[Longitudinal Semantic Fluency Decline and Lexical Simplification in
Preclinical Alzheimer's Disease: Associations with Dementia Conversion and
Biomarkers](/en/publications/xie26fluency/) has been accepted for publication
in the *Journal of Alzheimer's Disease*.

The study follows people's performance on a semantic fluency task (listing
as many animals as you can) over time. It asks whether declines in fluency,
and a shift toward simpler, more common words, track conversion to dementia
and Alzheimer's biomarkers before clinical symptoms appear. It builds on our
long-running work on [semantic fluency and memory search](/en/projects/snafu/).

The project was led by Wenli Xie, now a Data Scientist in Jet Vonk's lab at
the [UCSF Memory and Aging Center](https://memory.ucsf.edu/), together with
Jet Vonk, Kimberly Diggle Mueller (University of Wisconsin–Madison), Isabel
Elaine Allen and Christopher Seaman (UCSF), our alum Jeff Zemla (Syracuse
University), and Joseph Austerweil. Congratulations to Wenli, who started the
project as a master's student and carried it over the finish line!
