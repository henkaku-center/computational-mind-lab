The [webpage for our panel discussion](https://www.sice.or.jp/org/SSI2026/special_events.html)
at SSI2026, the annual symposium of the Society of Instrument and Control
Engineers (SICE) Systems and Information Division, is now live.

The special session, <span lang="ja">脳の多様性と変容を支えるシステム・情報技術：疾患進行モデリングと人間中心デザインの接点</span>
("Systems and information technologies supporting brain diversity and
change: where disease progression modeling meets human-centered design"),
takes place on **Wednesday, November 11, 2026, 13:30–14:30** at the
University of Tokyo.

Starting from the diversity of brains and cognition, and from the
experiences and values of the people being supported, the session rethinks
what support itself should look like. It brings together three perspectives:

- **Rua Williams** (Purdue University): critical disability studies,
  participatory research, and socio-technical system design
- **Joseph Austerweil** (Chiba Institute of Technology): [disease progression
  modeling](/en/projects/disease-progression/), which estimates the order in
  which a disease typically unfolds across a population and places each
  individual along it to estimate their stage
- **Sou Nobukawa** (Chiba Institute of Technology): biosignals such as EEG
  and pupil diameter as indicators for stratifying conditions and monitoring
  interventions; Nobukawa proposed the session and will moderate

The discussion will look at how these approaches can inform assessing an
individual's state, estimating disease stage, and evaluating responses to
intervention.
